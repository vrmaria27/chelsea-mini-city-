# Side streets: connecting the reconstructed areas

This milestone fills in the streets that connect the areas already rebuilt: King's Road, Sloane Square, Duke of York Square and the Saatchi Gallery, Peter Jones, Sloane Street and Lower Sloane Street. It stays inside the existing 700 m × 700 m map. The map is not enlarged, King's Road still ends at Markham Square, and nothing that was completed earlier was remodelled. The experience is still exploration only: no interiors and no gameplay.

![Plan: side-street buildings and walkable centrelines added in this milestone, by priority](sd-overview.png)

## 1. Audit and scope

The audit started from the streets meeting the reconstructed corridors, in the brief's priority order:
1. **P1** — around Peter Jones.
2. **P2** — King's Road, Duke of York Square, the Saatchi Gallery and Royal Avenue.
3. **P3** — around Sloane Square station and Lower Sloane Street.
4. **P4** — the remaining King's Road side streets.

For every candidate street, the audit took its OpenStreetMap centreline (`SIDE_STREETS` in `scripts/osm/process.mjs`) and decided how much of it to reconstruct:
- **Inside the map:** a street is reconstructed from its junction with a reconstructed corridor up to the point where it leaves the map. That point is 4 m inside the edge.
- **Junctions:** a street that reaches another reconstructed street ends at that junction.
- **Buildings at the edge:** a building is only modelled when its whole footprint is inside the map. Footprints cut by the edge stay plain massing. The rule is the same as for Sloane Street: no sliced reconstructions.

**Which buildings each street claims.** A street claims the buildings with a façade within 12 m of its centreline. Buildings already claimed by King's Road, Sloane Street, Lower Sloane Street, the Sloane Square perimeter or a landmark keep their existing models. Within 25 m of those corridors, the earlier OSM data is kept unchanged.

**Building data.** The OSM extract of the whole map was refetched on 2026-11-13 (`data/osm/sd-raw.json` and `sd-buildings.json`, from the `osm-sd` branch's workflow). Each building gets its storeys, roof storeys, materials, colour, building type, mapped shops and address from this extract.

**Reference photographs.** 420 Wikimedia Commons photographs were fetched by street name and by location (`scripts/refs/fetch-commons-sd.mjs` on the `references-sd` branch, with authors and licences in its `index.json`). Each photograph was assigned to a street by its title or its coordinates, and inspected street by street. They are cited as C:nnn. The photographs are modelling references only and are not committed to `main`.

## 2. How the streets are built

**Building configurations.** Each street-facing building gets its own configuration (`src/map/landmarks/sideStreetSpecs.ts`). It combines three sources:
- **The street-side profile, from the photographs.** The profile gives the shared character of the street side. The main types are:
  - stock-brick Georgian terraces over stucco ground floors with balconettes (Royal Avenue, Lincoln, Bourne and Cliveden);
  - red-brick "Pont Street Dutch" mansion terraces with stone bands and Dutch gables (Cadogan Gardens, Culford Gardens, Sloane Gardens and Blacklands Terrace);
  - red-brick mansion blocks with white bays (Burton Court and Sloane Court);
  - Victorian shopfronts with console brackets under red brick (Symons Street);
  - mews with garage doors (Pavilion Road and Holbein Mews);
  - Bywater Street's individually painted houses, with the colours cycled rather than matched house by house.
- **The building's own OSM data:** storeys, mansard roofs, materials and colour, shops, and frontage width. This is what makes neighbouring buildings differ where the data says they do.
- **Per-building overrides** for individually photographed buildings:
  - Cadogan Hall (Portland-stone arcade and round-headed windows);
  - the Sedding Street telephone exchange;
  - 3 Sloane Terrace;
  - Cheval Phoenix House;
  - Eaton Mansions;
  - Holy Trinity primary school;
  - the Tryon Street pub.

**Street surfaces.** Each street gets pavements and kerbs. The carriageway is fitted between the building lines (`Corridor.fitCarriageway`):
- **Centrelines:** these are densified, so straight two-vertex streets can be re-centred.
- **Narrow streets:** these may narrow to 4.6 m, or 3.6 m for Tryon Street.
- **Shared surfaces:** these are walked on the carriageway. They are used where the pavements are too narrow to model or absent: Pavilion Road (paved as a shared surface beside Symons Street, C:026, C:151), Holbein Mews, Tryon Street and Smith Terrace.
- **Pedestrian lane:** the Duke of York Square lane is pedestrian.

**Royal Avenue.** The centre is OSM `landcover=gravel` in a council park, and is drawn and walked as a gravel avenue. Previously it was a lawn with scattered decorative trees.

**Junctions.** Side-street mouths are crossed along the main street's pavement. Every overlapping street classifies a point, so obstacles and carriageways from either street still apply.

**End of each street.** A street that ends at the map edge or at non-reconstructed buildings shows "End of current area. *Street* is reconstructed as far as here…", with the existing way back. The message appears only within the end zone. Open-ended streets run on without a message.

**Legacy data.** The legacy development sites (`data/legacy/game-sites-v2.json`) and the generated sites are unchanged. They are analysed against the roads as they were before the side-street fits.

## 4. Performance

**Test setup.** Headless Chrome with SwiftShader (software rendering), 1400×900, on a 2-vCPU cloud machine. Absolute frame times say nothing about a Quest 3. Compare the triangles and draw calls instead.

**Levels of detail.** Corridor building models (King's Road, Sloane Street, Lower Sloane Street and the side streets) are now built at two levels:
- **Detailed models:** 462k triangles.
- **Simple extrusions:** 26k triangles, with the wall colour and window rows.

Both are split into a 10 × 10 grid. A tile is drawn in detail only within 90 m of the eye (55 m with *Low detail*). The miniature shows the simple level.

**Totals:**
- **Static mesh:** 225k triangles (328k on `main` before the LOD was added).

## 5. Checks

**Automated tests:** `tests/sideStreets.test.ts` checks that:
- every frontage is claimed and configured with a source;
- photographed streets are not *estimated*;
- models stay below sensible heights and out of the carriageway;
- every side street can be walked end to end;
- closed streets warn at their end, and open ones do not;
- Royal Avenue is a walkable gravel avenue;
- the legacy sites are intact;
- the LOD's simple level is under one eighth of the detailed one.

**Full check:** `npm run check` (typecheck, 75 tests, build) passes.

## 6. Remaining gaps

**Still estimated** (no usable photograph found):
- Walpole Street (8 buildings);
- Whittaker Street (1 building).

**Partly observed, house by house:** most buildings are *partly observed*. Each one shares its street's photographed character, and its storeys, width and shops come from OSM. Individual details are not matched house by house (porches, bay windows, railings, door colours, Bywater Street's exact colours).

**Simplified:**
- Mansard roofs come from OSM `roof:levels`. Where OSM records roof storeys, the mansard replaces the profile's gables.
- Cadogan Hall's tracery is simplified, and its clock tower is outside the map. Eaton Mansions has no corner turret.
- Royal Avenue's tree rows are not drawn: no sourced tree positions are in the extract.
- Street furniture on side streets is limited to OSM-mapped lamps, bollards, trees and post boxes.

**Not reconstructed:**
- Streets beyond the map edge.
- Buildings whose footprints the edge cuts.
- The Wellington Square loop.

## 7. Quest 3 checklist (not yet tested on a headset)

1. In the miniature, check that the new streets read clearly and that frame pacing is steady. Toggle *Low detail* and compare.
2. At street level, walk Symons Street → Pavilion Road → Cadogan Gardens → Sloane Square. Check:
   - no stutter when tiles switch between simple and detailed models;
   - no floating or buried geometry.
3. Walk down Pavilion Road and Tryon Street on the shared surface. Check that the eye height feels right at the kerbs.
4. Walk Royal Avenue's gravel and the Duke of York Square lane back to King's Road.
5. Approach the ends of Lincoln Street, Holbein Place and Walpole Street:
   - the message should appear only near the end;
   - *Return* should work with the controller ray.
6. Look at Bywater Street and Cadogan Hall up close:
   - no z-fighting on the painted house panels;
   - window glass visible through them.
7. Check that Sloane Square, King's Road, Sloane Street and Lower Sloane Street look and behave as before.
